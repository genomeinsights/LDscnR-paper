Where the C-score benchmark stands

Four selection regimes, forty genomes, and six corrections — three of which reversed a conclusion I had already reported.

Data bgs5, 4 cells × 10 env × 2 arms Rows 318 / 80 genomes Updated 31 Aug 2026 Also covers pilots, 796-bundle enrichment, permutation runs

What is actually being compared

This framing was nearly overstated once, so it leads.

Both arms are region-based. They call the same scoring function: ld_regions() to cluster the selected markers, the same l_min filter, the same evaluate_ors() against the same truth table. The only thing that differs is which markers get selected — C ≥ tau versus q < alpha, computed on identical p-values.

There is no single-SNP arm, by design. So the result speaks to the selection statistic, not to whether LD-aggregation beats marker-wise testing. That second question would need a separate arm and has not been run.

Corrections that changed a conclusion

Marked entries reversed or removed something already reported. The rest tightened a claim without overturning it.

Reversed a reported result

Pseudo-replication in the standard errors

Pooling 2 engines × 2 l_min × 10 environments quadrupled n, but those four rows are one genome measured four ways. At genome level V0.5_c1 went from t = −3.2 to −1.7 — from result to non-result. It had been reported as a result.

Reversed my prediction

Common support ran against the C-score

pr_auc() integrates to whatever recall a sweep reached. I predicted this favoured C, since C sweeps a 1.6–2.7× larger candidate set. Measured, BH alpha attains higher maximum recall in nearly every row: a larger candidate set does not buy recall when the extra markers form no scorable region. Correcting it moved every cell in C's favour.

Removed a stated finding

"BGS halves PR-AUC" did not survive replication

That came from a single environment on two cells. On ten environments the ratio is ≈1.5×, not ≈2.5×. The old bundles were fine — they reproduce to within 0.01 — so it was env1 being unrepresentative, not a convention problem.

Mislabelled axis

Power is governed by c, not V

V is selection variance, so larger V is weaker selection — but alpha AUC spans 17-fold at identical V, and V2_c1 is the second easiest cell, not the low-power one I called it. Panels now order by measured difficulty and carry the value.

Wrong quantity named

Focal QTN, not nearest

max_LD_with_QTN is the maximum r² over a chromosome's QTN, and the focal QTN differs from the nearest for 56.2% of markers. The pruning manuscript's Figure 2 caption carries the same error and still needs fixing.

Stale settings

min_r2 and distance_threshold

The sim scripts were pinned at a fixed min_r2 = 0.2 and 5e5 while the bundles used the derived ρ = 0.5 floor and 1e5. Both now match, and both are pinned by measurement — 1e5 reproduces the stored grm_markers exactly (12,242).

What ld_w actually tracks

From the pooled 20-chromosome genome, V0.5_c1_env1.

ld_w is substantially a low-recombination detector rather than a signal detector: in V0.5_c1, Spearman ρ = −0.46 per marker against recombination rate, rising to −0.78 at 500 kb windows. The aggregation gap is the point — per-marker rate is noisy relative to the LD it produces, so the marker-level number understates the dependence.

Those figures are stable across environments but not across cells. Over all ten environments of V0.5_c1 the window-scale ρ spans just −0.772 to −0.803 (median −0.780, and the published env1 value of −0.778 is essentially the median). Between cells it moves far more: V1_c1.5 env2 gives −0.36 (nobgs) and −0.62 (bgs). So the strength of the relationship is a property of the regime, and the number above should be read as V0.5_c1's, not as the method's.

It is also not an artefact of bgs5's coarse recombination map. The map-rebuild pilots carry 1.5–2.1% of loci at zero recombination against bgs5's 36–42%, and on the matched cell and environment their window-scale correlations are as strong or stronger — −0.19 to −0.53 (nobgs) and −0.66 to −0.81 (bgs), against bgs5's −0.36 and −0.62. Twenty-fold fewer cold spots, same relationship.

Markers in strong LD with a causal variant (r² > 0.8) sit at a median log1p(rec) of 0.00, against 0.63 genome-wide. They are not merely enriched in low-recombination regions; they are concentrated at the floor.

Checked 31 Aug — the cold spots are real

A third of the map sits at identical genetic positions, and that is not a bug

Adjacent loci frequently share a truncated map position — 701 blocks covering 33.8% of markers here — which briefly looked like a discretisation artefact manufacturing zero-recombination blocks. It is not. Within a block the genetic span is exactly zero for 46.1% and a median of 2.2 × 10⁻⁵ cM for the rest; the largest span observed is 1.96 truncation bins, so the truncation is barely binding. rec_rate is exactly zero at 92.3% of these markers against 0.2% elsewhere. Recombination genuinely does not happen between them.

Dropping them as a robustness check moves the correlation from −0.46 to −0.33 per marker and −0.78 to −0.61 at window scale — but that is removing the cold spots themselves, which is where local LD lives and where ld_w is supposed to fire. It bounds how much of the relationship comes from cold spots specifically; it is not a de-biased estimate, and the un-dropped figures are the ones to quote.

But several causal variants sit at background ld_w — real, locally unlinked, and unreachable at any C threshold. That is the blind spot, and it is the mechanism by which C loses in the one cell where it does: where power is high enough that BH finds those loci anyway, discarding them costs more than the LD weighting gains.

The C-score's thresholds transfer essentially unchanged

Because they are decay-relative, and because the floor b that sets them is the same: min_r2 at ρ=0.5 is 0.561 on bgs5 against 0.556–0.563 on the pilots, and r2_link at ρ=0.75 is 0.342 against 0.341–0.346. Differences under 1%. The selection advantage holds too — at matched budget, C's own k against the top k by p-value, paired within dataset, C is more precise in 11 of 12 non-tied pilot datasets, mean gain +0.061, Wilcoxon p = 0.0015.

The enrichment is not geometry

Selecting markers by ld_w alone — a genotype-only statistic that never sees the phenotype — enriches 1.4–6.2× (not MAF-matched — roughly halved when it is; see the corrected table below) for markers tagging a causal variant at the top 10%. Against a rotation null that preserves ld_w's autocorrelation and the tagging pattern while breaking their alignment, 18 of 18 datasets are significant (null mean 1.00, observed 1.40–6.23). Checked rather than assumed, because a containment-style statistic over clusters elsewhere in the project does collapse against size-matched controls; a per-marker statistic cannot fail that way.

At equal budget the three rules separate by regime: the C-score is best at small k and then stops — it is exactly zero for >98% of markers, so beyond its support it has no ranking to offer — while ld_w alone holds its enrichment furthest, out-performing the p-value at large budgets in three of four pilot maps.

Qualified — the truth definition flatters ld_w

The enrichment is real, but it is not a detection claim

The enrichment above is scored against the truth “this marker is in LD (r² above threshold) with a QTN”. ld_w is itself an LD statistic, so that truth shares machinery with the statistic being scored: high-ld_w markers are in strong LD with many things, causal variants included, partly by construction. The rotation null does not remove this — it breaks the alignment between ld_w and the tagging pattern, not the definitional overlap between them.

Scored instead against a truth that shares nothing with either statistic — bp distance to the nearest driving QTN, causal variants excluded — the ordering changes. In the sim-parsing session’s arbitration, ld_w alone reaches fold 2.35 and recall 23%, against the association statistic’s 4.14 / 41%: ld_w is the weakest of the candidates at recovering the QTN neighbourhood on its own.

Both results can hold. The enrichment numbers are not wrong, and tagging is the operationally relevant truth for a scan whose unit is “markers that tag a QTN”. But the numbers should not be quoted as evidence that ld_w detects causal variation, and the “out-performs the p-value at large budgets” comparison above inherits the same favourable truth and has not been re-tested against a distance-based one.

One property of ld_w does survive independent testing and is worth keeping: it is not a frequency proxy. Substituting MAF for ld_w as a multiplier makes the association statistic strictly worse (p < 1e−4 across both datasets, rank-normalised too), while F × ld_w beats F × MAF decisively. Whatever ld_w carries is confounded with frequency but not reducible to it.

Method trap, recorded

The C-score cannot be scored as a ranker

Testing it with a genome-wide AUC shows it losing badly to the raw p-value, 0.527 against 0.675. That test is invalid: with C zero across >98% of the genome, the AUC is pinned near 0.5 by ties whatever the selection quality — the tell was auc_C sitting at 0.50–0.57 in every dataset. At matched budget the sign reverses. C is a selector, not a ranker, and anything evaluating it as the latter will reproduce this.

Not a convergence

The two results point different ways across these four cells

It is tempting to read "enrichment rises as the base rate falls" as the marker-level version of "the C-score's relative gain rises as power falls". Across the four cells they run opposite: enrichment is ρ = +0.80 against power and ρ = −0.60 against the relative C-gain, and V0.5_c1 is both the second-best cell for enrichment and the only one where the C-score loses.

Neither result is wrong. One measures how much purer ld_w makes a marker set, the other whether C beats BH at region-level detection, and both can hold at once. They are simply not two independent statistics agreeing, and should not be cited together as though they were.

Mechanism withdrawn

My explanation for the divergence did not survive testing

I attributed it to the tagging base rate running against power — ρ = −0.80, so "rises as base rate falls" would mean "rises as power rises". That correlation is four cell means and nothing more. Joined at genome level and tested within cells, where the between-cell structure cannot contribute, base rate against power is ρ = −0.016 pooled over 80 genomes and 4 of 8 within-cell correlations are negative. There is no such axis. The divergence stands; the reason I gave for it does not.

A candidate that does survive: background LD, not power

Background LD b tracks the tagging base rate, and unlike the power story it holds beneath the cell means. Over 235 bundles: ρ = +1.00 across the four cell means, 7 of 8 within-cell correlations positive (median +0.39), and ρ = +0.32 pooled after removing cell and arm means. ld_w is a local-LD statistic, so where background LD is high everything is correlated with everything — local LD carries less information, and the tagging label becomes easier to earn at the same time. One quantity plausibly drives both sides without power entering at all.

But the base rates it is measured against need a decay-relative threshold. The 796-bundle figures use a fixed cut of r² ≥ 0.27, and in V0.5_c2 background LD is b = 0.42 — above the cut. An unlinked marker there earns the tagging label from background alone, so part of the b-to-base-rate relationship is definitional rather than biological. Scored at each cell's own score_thresholds() value instead, the same cell reads 6.0% rather than 21.7%, and the spread across cells is 1.0–6.3% rather than 1.2–45.1%. The relationship survives the change; its reported magnitudes do not.

Two things it does establish

The degenerate-estimator regime is generic, not a quirk of small samples. At the top 1%, V0.5_c1 bgs has a median fold of exactly 0.00 across 100 bundles and V2_c1 nobgs an IQR of 0–16.3: at a 0.7–2.7% base rate the top 1% contains no tagging marker in most bundles. That is the at-scale version of the same failure that produced a fold of 0.00 from a single pilot dataset, and it is the reason fixed-small-budget statistics are unusable on this data.

Withdrawn — retracted at source

This paragraph previously read “BGS bites at marker level but not at region level — in V2_c1 the enrichment collapses 7.56 → 2.75 under background selection, with recall 73% → 27%.” That number has been retracted by the session that produced it and should not have survived here; I was told of the retraction and corrected it in conversation but left the document unchanged. It is one cell and one arm pair, and the two arms share zero QTN positions, so it confounds background selection with an independent draw.

A 128-bundle test in V0.5_c1 finds no BGS effect on enrichment fold (2.63 vs 2.51, Wilcoxon p = 0.634), with the sign flipping across all four map variants. What does survive from the same bundles is a different and better-supported claim: the arms carry the same driving QTN (1.75 vs 1.75, p = 0.97) but recall differs, 57% vs 79% (p = 0.0023). BGS costs recall, not enrichment. Roughly 40% of recall variance tracks how many tagging markers exist, which accounts for part of that gap but not all of it; the residual is arm-associated and deliberately unnamed.

The original paragraph’s conclusion is unaffected and stands: whatever BGS does here does not propagate to the C-vs-BH comparison. The shift in the C-gap from nobgs to bgs is at most 0.027 (V1_c1.5) and only −0.010 in V2_c1 — consistent with both arms reading the same p-values, so most of the effect is common-mode and cancels.

tau_C = 0.05 is principled, and that matters more than the win

C is the fraction of (rho, q*, alpha) cells in which a marker cleared BH at 0.05 — so tau_C = 0.05 means significant at the conventional threshold in at least 5% of the analyses. Both rho and q* are bounded on [0,1], so that fraction is a canonical quantity rather than a tuned constant. The integration is genuinely broad: all 400 EMMAX grid cells yield regions and 399 contain a QTN, so C is averaging rather than being set by a corner.

That property is exactly what C² lacks. Its grid runs over tau and l_min, and l_min has no natural upper bound — a C² computed over l_min 2–10 is not comparable with one over 40–100, so a cut like c2 >= 0.9 means nothing fixed.

Measured — the principle holds, the benefit does not

tau_C = 0.05 does not beat alpha = 0.05

The argument above for tau_C = 0.05 being canonical rather than tuned still stands. What does not stand is any implication that adopting it helps. Paired over 160 arms at l_min = 5:

mean d = +0.0027   median d = +0.0000   C > alpha in 79/151 (52%)   sign p = 0.63   wilcoxon p = 0.65

A coin flip, with a median difference of exactly zero. The C-score’s advantage over alpha becomes real only at tau ≥ 0.5 (73% of arms, p = 3e−7) and tau = 0.7 (86%, p = 2e−17) — that is, only where the threshold is fitted rather than derived. The head-to-head at l_min = 5 pooled agrees: 76/151 (50%), p = 1.0.

What survives is narrow and both clauses matter equally: integrated across thresholds the C-score ranks modestly better (pooled PR-AUC, C > alpha in 179/313 arms, sign p = 0.013), and it beats alpha at no threshold you would deploy. Stated without the second clause the first reads as a win; it is not one.

Mechanism, from a paired swap test run independently in the sim-parsing session: reweighting a p-value by ld_w reshuffles a median 32% of the top-k and nets ~1 marker nearer a driving QTN out of a top-k of ~1,400 at 50–100 kb, and exactly zero at 10 kb, which is tagging scale. A reweighting of that size predicts mean PR-AUC differences of the +0.001 to +0.009 magnitude measured here and a sign test at chance. The two results agree from opposite directions.

What the null side can and cannot do

The gate is decisive — 17 observed regions against a surrogate median of 0, with 4–8% of surrogates producing any region at all. But q_R cannot rank, and raising B will not fix it: every observed region has n_null_ge = 0, so all sit at the p-floor 1/(1+B), and B = 1000 merely lowers the floor and leaves them tied. It is a screening test, not a ranking one.

Recommendation withdrawn

Ranking by s_R is ranking by size

I suggested ranking surviving regions by s_R rather than C². That was wrong in a way worth recording: s_R sums C over member markers, so it is size-inflated by construction. Spearman(s_R, size) = +0.84, and residualising log(s_R) on log(size) drops the ranking AUC from 0.923 to 0.582 — near chance. At matched budget s_R and size are identical in 60 of 64 comparisons. s_R, C² and size are three routes to one ordering; size is the simplest, and the rule should be described as "report the largest LD blocks" rather than dressed up.

Reframes the threshold

tau_C is not the operative cut; l_min is

On its own tau_C = 0.05 admits a great deal of noise: the modal C-score cluster is a single isolated marker, 59% are singletons, and essentially none of those contain a QTN. The l_min = 3 operating point removes three quarters of all clusters (1738 → 458) at a cost of 0.1 QTN. So the working rule is tau_C and an l_min, and the second does more of the work than the first — worth stating plainly rather than presenting tau_C as the threshold that matters.

l_min = 3 is dominated

At tau = 0.05, averaged over environments: going l_min 3 → 5 cuts regions from 22.9 to 13.3 while losing 0.1 QTN (4.6 → 4.5) and lifts precision 0.24 → 0.39. l_min = 10 keeps 4.2 of 4.6 in a third of the regions. The benchmark default is a poor operating point for both arms.
